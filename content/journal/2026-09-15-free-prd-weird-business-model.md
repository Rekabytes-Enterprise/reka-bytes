We get asked this a lot: if the first conversation is free, what stops people
from picking our brains and disappearing?

Short answer: nothing. And that's the point.

## The pitch meeting nobody likes

If you've ever had an app idea, you've probably had this experience. You
describe it to a developer or an agency, and somewhere in the first hour the
conversation turns into a sales call. You learn more about _their_ process than
about _your_ product, and the meeting ends with a vague quote and a feeling
you've been pitched to.

We don't want to be that meeting. So we flipped the incentives: **the first
conversation costs you nothing, and our job in it is to give you an honest take
on scope, timeline, and budget** — even when the honest take is "you don't need
to hire anyone for this yet."

That feels risky. It isn't, really. When we tell a founder the truth about
their idea early, they remember it — even when it costs us the project.

## What you actually get

If you come to us with an idea, here's the path, and what each step costs:

1. **Free consultation.** A call or an email thread. You describe the idea; we
   ask the annoying questions — who uses it, what breaks without it, what does
   "done" mean. You leave with a realistic sense of size and cost.
2. **Free concept mockup.** With the consultation, we sketch the app as a few
   screens. Not a design system — a picture of the thing you're describing, so
   the rest of the conversation happens about something concrete.
3. **A full PRD from RM 150.** If you decide you want the spec, you get a
   complete product requirements document: functional requirements,
   non-functional requirements, error handling, and a test plan you can use to
   sign off on the work. PDF or Markdown. Yours to keep — ours or any AI agent's
   to build with.

The PRD step is the one people find strange. Writing a real spec takes us time,
so why price it at the cost of a nice dinner? Because the spec is where we
demonstrate what we actually do. Anyone can promise quality; a document that
lists every failure mode and how it's handled **is** quality, in writing.

## Why the free tier is honest, not bait

Here's what we're betting on: a founder who gets real value before paying
anything — a straight answer, a usable mockup, a spec that's correct — does
not need to be sold later. They compare us to everyone who gated that
conversation behind an invoice.

And when a free consult ends with "you don't need us yet," that's a good
outcome too. The right project, at the right time, is how we keep work we
believe in. Builders can smell when a studio is excited about the wrong thing;
we'd rather be the studio that says no early.
